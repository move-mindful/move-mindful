The problem

Your phone is training your body into a posture you don’t want.

Every hour spent looking down at a screen reinforces forward head posture, rounded shoulders, and a stiff upper back — until that hunched position begins to feel normal.

Eventually, you may notice:

A more visible hump at the base of your neck
Your head drifting forward in photos
Rounded shoulders and a tight chest
A stiff or increasingly curved upper back
Difficulty standing tall without straining
Restricted shoulder or overhead movement
Neck and upper-back tension after sitting

Why what you’re trying isn’t working

Reminding yourself to sit up straight It lasts until you stop thinking about it, because the muscles that should hold you there haven’t been trained to.
Stretching when you feel tight It can feel good for a moment, but stretching alone doesn’t build the strength your body needs to stay upright.
Waiting until you have time for a class Long workouts are hard to fit in, and most aren’t designed for bodies shaped by sitting and screens.

Simply pulling your shoulders back won’t address the entire pattern. To create meaningful change, you need to restore movement where your body has become stiff and strengthen the muscles responsible for supporting a more upright position.

The method

Twelve minutes a day can begin to change the pattern.

Each routine blends four things your body needs to stop feeling pulled forward, with Ayla guiding you through every movement.

Mobilize

Restore movement where your body has stiffened: your upper back, shoulders, neck, and hips.

Strengthen

Build the back, core, and shoulder-blade muscles that support you, so standing tall isn’t something you force.

Support

Therapeutic techniques and simple props, like a folded blanket, help your body release instead of brace.

Awareness

Mindful cues teach you to feel the difference, so a more upright posture starts to feel natural.

What it’s designed to help you do
